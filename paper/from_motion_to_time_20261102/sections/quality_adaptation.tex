\section{Does learned routing add value beyond box geometry?}
\label{sec:quality-adaptation}
The geometric extension combines the frozen fused, RGB, and event estimates with apparent expansion from supplied box history. We fit inverse TTC from width, height, and square root of area over the preceding 650\,ms, using median pair slopes and a query-time intercept. A 688-parameter gate uses fit quality and expert agreement to weight the four inverse-TTC estimates; the delivered adapter averages three independently seeded gate outputs in seconds. Its fit uses 2,608 queries from 24 road recordings and Slider-500. Appendix~\ref{app:quality-complete} gives the geometry, 33 input fields, training recipe, and fallback rules.

\subsection{Matched experts, timing, and coverage}
We compare the original model, geometry alone, geometry with original-model fallback, a fixed half-and-half inverse-TTC mixture, and the released router. All use the same saved causal inputs and 434 corrected target correspondences. The fixed weight is specified before calculating these comparisons. Geometry is available on 424 queries, including 357 of 365 road queries. Table~\ref{tab:revision-geometry} reports both available coverage and identical geometry-valid support; an abstention never silently changes a comparator's denominator. We retain signed outputs and the shared finite emission policy. Organizer-style interpolation appears only in the separate supplementary analysis.

On common support, geometry alone has lower median error but large outliers, yielding mean error 1.158\,s. A fixed mixture reduces MAE to 0.289\,s, compared with the router's 0.304\,s; the fixed-minus-router paired MAE difference is $-0.0146$\,s, with a 95\% recording-bootstrap interval of $[-0.1704,0.0943]$\,s. The road-only comparison changes that conclusion: the router reduces MAE from the fixed mixture's 0.221 to 0.159\,s, with a fixed-minus-router difference of 0.062\,s and a recording-bootstrap interval of $[0.014,0.120]$\,s. Excluding Slider-750 also favors the router, by 0.057\,s with interval $[0.008,0.111]$\,s across nine recordings. These are descriptive comparisons on development-used data.

Thus adding geometry explains much of the pooled improvement, while learned routing adds value on the road subset by controlling unreliable geometric estimates. The evidence supports this conditional benefit; the pooled result alone would conceal it. The original model's locally reconstructed organizer-style score still disagrees for Slider-750. Appendix~\ref{par:local-score-correspondence} gives the exact discrepancy, without attributing it to an unverified physical cause. Independent recordings with detector-produced boxes are needed to test whether the routing benefit persists under new geometry errors.
