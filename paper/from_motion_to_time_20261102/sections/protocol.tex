\section{Training and evaluation}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Grouped out-of-fold training and final refitting}

Five rotations of eight-sequence blocks generate leakage-controlled inputs for the upper stages. In each rotation, twenty-four groups fit the four producers and two PAIR heads, eight different groups fit the context refiners and normalizers, and the remaining eight groups generate out-of-fold features. A query group never contributes to supervised fitting of the models that generate its upper-stage features. \evidence{M23}

I pool PHASE17 RGB and event features across the out-of-fold rotations to train the context heads and normalizers. Those models then generate the PHASE20 features, expressed through the 340-coordinate container, for fusion-head training. Rotations pool named fields with shared semantics, while learned hidden coordinates stay within their originating models. Targets join the data only after out-of-fold feature generation. \evidence{M23}

Inference executes one all-TRAIN40 chain comprising the four producers, two PAIR heads, two context refiners, and fusion head. Auxiliary producers see twenty-four groups per rotation, whereas deployed producers see all forty. This stage-wise exposure difference can shift the upper-stage feature distribution after refitting. Earlier development use also remains relevant to architecture-selection claims.

Training comprised 690,934 auxiliary endpoint updates and 58,660 final-head updates across forty-five fits. Final PAIR endpoints used 11,264 updates; each context model and the final head used 12,044. Schedulers and warmup scaled with the fitting population. Update counts describe optimization exposure rather than wall-clock GPU effort. \evidence{L45}

Producer training combines relational DINO teacher signals, foreground geometry supervision, and task-specific adaptation. The deployed chain no longer evaluates the generic teacher, but its supervision remains part of the training recipe.


\subsection{Metrics and target coordinates}
TTC error in seconds, inverse-TTC error, benchmark phase error, and detection of urgent risk measure different aspects of behaviour. I use the signed benchmark phase
\begin{equation}
\phi(T)=-\log\!\left(1-\frac{0.1\,\mathrm{s}}{T}\right),\qquad T<0\;\text{or}\;T>0.1\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{equation}
Negative TTC denotes recession. The interval $[0,0.1]$\,s is outside this transform's domain. The official benchmark scorer defines motion-in-depth (MiD) error as
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{MiD}(\widehat T,T)=10^4\left|\log\left(1-\frac{0.1}{T}\right)
 -\log\left(1-\frac{0.1}{\widehat T}\right)\right|
 =10^4|\phi(\widehat T)-\phi(T)|.
\end{equation}
MiD measures phase displacement. At large positive $T$, the first-order approximation $\phi(T)\simeq0.1/T$ compresses differences in seconds, while sensitivity rises near the positive domain boundary. I therefore report MAE in seconds alongside MiD. \evidence{M10,M15}

The scorer groups target TTC into four right-closed intervals: crucial $c:(0,3]$\,s, small $s:(3,6]$\,s, large $l:(6,10]$\,s, and negative $n:(-10,0]$\,s. The grouping includes zero in the negative band, although the phase transform is undefined at zero; metric validity is checked separately. Targets outside these bands do not contribute to the band-weighted score.

The scorer computes the band-weighted score
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{overall\_MiD}=0.5\,\mathrm{MiD}_{c}+0.3\,\mathrm{MiD}_{s}
                     +0.1\,\mathrm{MiD}_{l}+0.1\,\mathrm{MiD}_{n},
\end{equation}
and a sample-weighted mean MiD. Missing bands remain missing, without weight redistribution. The complete band-weighted score requires targets in all four bands. The evaluation fixes the scorer by SHA-256 and tests a perfect four-band example, a hand-computed crucial-band error, and an absent negative band. \evidence{M15}

Metric reductions retain failures explicitly. The wrapper records invalid predictions by band and leaves its strict mean unavailable when any row is invalid. The official failure rate differs from the false-negative rate for urgent collision warnings. I therefore report prediction coverage, invalid-MiD count, and risk recall with their respective metrics. A finite saturated TTC may still carry an unsupported-measurement flag.

\subsection{Corrected local targets and later development cohorts}
The 434-query competition inference snapshot originally supported only coverage and model disagreement. The subsequent target-source audit corrected the public assignment for two road recordings and recovered eleven previously absent targets, giving 434 supported targets. The local error comparison reports query-weighted MAE and mean relative error. Both original and adapted columns apply the organizer-described interpolation of negative predictions between valid outputs for scoring only; their archived prediction files retain the signed values unchanged. The original and adapted files contain two and one negative values, respectively. Nine of ten sequence relative-error rows reproduce the organizer-provided original result after rounding, while Slider-750 remains discrepant. The corrected local table therefore does not constitute a new official score. \evidence{N01}

The cadence experiment has a separate population: 2,531 queries from twenty-four road recordings and 77 from Slider-500. Its primary summaries average recordings equally within each domain, preserve signed predictions without clipping, and use relative denominator $\max(|T|,10^{-6})$. Availability is reported independently of paired error, including the frozen F0 route's one missing Slider output. The protocol requires preserved coverage, no worsening of MAE or relative error in either domain, and an improvement in at least one metric. A failed fixed 149-query screen remains a failure even when the larger-population aggregate improves. \evidence{N02,N03}

The final quality-router fit also uses 2,608 development queries and excludes the eight competition road recordings and Slider-750/1000 from optimizer updates. Earlier experiments had already inspected those recordings, so this exclusion does not establish independent generalization. The later paired refit and canonical-cadence runs are separate model variants. F0 passes its numerical development criteria, but the router/refit gates fail and native accuracy conservation remains unverified. Neither replaces the delivered quality adapter. \evidence{N01--N03}

\subsection{Independence and paired comparisons}
Complete sequences form the units of separation and resampling. A sample-weighted sequence bootstrap carries all rows from each sampled group into the replicate and recomputes the ratio of summed losses to summed counts; sequence-macro summaries weight groups equally. Both reductions inherit any development exposure. Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation-details} gives the corresponding lineage, causality, and latency checks. \evidence{M15--M20}

Three head seeds quantify optimization variability conditional on their parents; they do not independently replicate the upstream representation. The additional transfer bootstrap is post hoc: it resamples saved paired predictions without changing a model, split, or historical interval (Appendix~\ref{app:derived-transfer}).

For canonical cadence, 10,000 paired road-recording bootstrap replicates with seed 7 estimate descriptive intervals over the twenty-four development recordings. The intervals retain the cohort's historical exposure. Slider-500 is one recording reused from the screen and receives no between-recording confidence interval. A separate eight-fold, three-seed, two-arm router comparison comprises 48 fits; those fits do not create 48 independent datasets or erase upstream exposure. \evidence{N02,N03}
